How the compensation chamber sets the loop operating point

The CC is the only saturated volume in the loop, so its temperature fixes the pressure level everything else hangs from. Move the three inputs and watch where the cycle sits on the saturation curve, how much capillary head is left, and whether the returning liquid can carry the parasitic heat leak away. All quantities are reduced (divided by their critical-point values); the closure is a qualitative corresponding-states fit, not a design correlation.

Governing relations and assumptions

(1)ln Pr = A(1 − 1/Tr) ,  A = 7
(2)P7,8 = Psat(T8) — the CC fixes the loop pressure level
(3)P1 − P9 = ΔPGV + ΔPVL + ΔPCOND + ΔPLL + ΔPWICK
(4)ΔPcap ≤ ΔPcap,max = 2σ(T8)/rp
(5)T1 = Tsat(P1) ,  T1 − T8 ≈ ΔPext(dT/dP)sat
(6)ṁ = Q/hfg ,  ΔPv ∝ ṁ/ρv(T8)
(7)Q = UA2φ(T5 − Tsink) ⇒ L2φ/Lc
(8)Qleak = ṁ cp,l ΔTsub — CC energy balance
(9)ΔTsub,av = (T5 − Tsink)[1 − exp(−NTUsub)]
(10)hfg* = θ0.38, σ* = θ1.26, ρl* = θ0.28, θ = (1−Tr)/0.3

Closure: ρv* ∝ Pr/Tr (ideal gas), μl* = exp[2.5(1/Tr − 1/0.7)], all ΔP laminar (∝ ṁμ/ρ). Segments 2–3 and 3–4 carry vapour superheat; 5–6 and 6–7 carry liquid subcooling. Gravity, non-condensable gas, transient charge redistribution and wick deprime hysteresis are all omitted. The pressure and temperature scales of the main P–T figure are magnified about the operating point, following the standard schematic construction. This is a study aid: trends are meaningful, absolute numbers are not.
